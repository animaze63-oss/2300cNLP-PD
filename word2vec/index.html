        What am I looking at?

        1

        Word2Vec

        Word2Vec learns a short list of numbers (a vector) for every word by training a tiny neural network to predict which words appear near each other. Words used in similar contexts — doctor and nurse, paris and london — end up with similar vectors. That is the distributional hypothesis: “you shall know a word by the company it keeps.”

        2

        Cosine similarity

        Similarity is the cosine of the angle between two vectors: cos(u,v) = u·v / (|u||v|). It is 1 when they point the same way, 0 when unrelated. Every vector here is pre-normalised to length 1, so the score is just a dot product — the page compares your word against the whole vocabulary in milliseconds.

        3

        PCA projection

        Vectors live in 100 dimensions, which we can't draw. Principal component analysis finds the directions along which the plotted words vary the most and projects onto them. The % on each axis is the share of that variance kept. It is a flattened view: always trust the neighbour list, which is computed in the full space.
